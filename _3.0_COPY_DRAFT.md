for in the first breath. No "most software," no four-clause buildups, no
"measured, not promised" throat-clearing. If a sentence is defending itself,
it's wrong.

**Heading:** The problem the whole field quietly gave up on.

> differently, so the same program gives you a slightly different answer the
> moment it moves. Fixing that across machines sits in the "unsolved" column
> everywhere — on roadmaps, in whitepapers, never shipped.
>
> We solved it, and not with faster silicon. With a contract. Same bytes on the
> card, on the CPU, and out of the compiler that built itself.

## VOICE RULES (so every page matches)

- Announce the thing. Don't set up the thing.
- One idea per sentence. Break the essay sentences into shots.
- Concrete nouns. "Your laptop," "a receipt," "same bytes." Not "systems," "frameworks," "solutions."
- Numbers earn their place or they're cut.
- No negative parallelism ("not just X, but Y"), no rule-of-three padding, no em-dash pileups.
- The reader came to find out what it's FOR. Answer that first; explain the mechanism second.
